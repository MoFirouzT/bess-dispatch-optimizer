## R2.1e IMPLEMENTED (gate green): de-levelled target; a null at the default window, a real gain at two years (2026-07-28)

The model-change phase R2.1d sequenced itself before. Predict a standardized target `z = (price - level)/scale` from a trailing 168 h window ending at `t-24 h`, invert with `price = level + scale*z`. Because level and scale are known constants at prediction time and scale > 0, the inverse is a strictly increasing affine map and R2.1's marginal coverage is **inherited, not re-derived**. **Spec: [`docs/specs/R2.1e-target-normalization.md`](specs/R2.1e-target-normalization.md) (Implemented, every build task and acceptance box ticked). Full suite 373 passed; ruff/format/mypy(43)/lint-imports(4 KEPT)/docs-lint(57) clean. No optimizer change.**

- **The headline gate improves, and an intermediate reading of it was wrong.** On the shipped 365-day configuration, hour-of-day conditional coverage moves **0.0653 to 0.0498** (cqr), a 24% reduction, with marginal coverage held (0.9003 to 0.9008) and mean width *narrower* (138.3 to 134.8). An earlier draft of this entry called it a null (0.0653 to 0.0622); that figure came from a bundle including the cyclical encoding, which the decomposition below shows is harmful. Dropping it, which is what ships, makes the gain about 4x larger. Measuring a bundle measures the bundle.
- **The real result is that normalization flips the training-window sweep.** At identical test days, pinball raw/normalized: 90d **3.574/3.761**, 180d **3.674/3.796**, 365d **3.532/3.419**, 730d **3.711/3.405**, width at 730d **106.0 to 93.1**. Under the raw target 730 days was the *worst* window R2.1d measured; under normalization it is the *best*, with the narrowest intervals. Clean crossover at 365 days: de-levelling **hurts** short windows (already roughly stationary, so it only adds estimation noise) and **helps** long ones (which carry the regime drift it exists to remove). **This answers the gas-crisis question the session opened with: crisis history is harmful as training data under a raw target and useful under a de-levelled one.**
- **The cyclical season encoding was measured again and is NOT shipped.** The phase's premise was that R2.1d's full-year rolling window would rescue the encoding R2.1 reverted. It does not: adding it costs pinball at both windows (3.419 to 3.509 at 365d; 3.411 to 3.532 at 730d) and raises quantile crossing (2 to 37 clipped at 730d). `season_encoding` stays `"month"`; the R2.1 revert now rests on a measurement rather than on a 4-month-window artifact. Rolling-stat features are a genuine trade (best conditional coverage measured, 0.0575 at 730d, but worse pinball) and are also left off by default.
- **A pre-existing defect found and fixed en route: quantile crossing.** `lower <= point <= upper`, asserted in the R2.1 spec *and* in `formulation-r2.md` §R2.1, is violated by the shipped CQR model, which fits three independent quantile learners and conformalizes only the lower/upper pair. **174 of 32,665** real-data predictions out of order at 365d; 10 of 30 synthetic seeds affected. `test_interval_ordering` had passed on one lucky seed and is now swept over the failing ones (confirmed red without the fix). **`lower > upper` never occurred, so the interval always carried its guarantee and no coverage number moves**; only `.point`, which is the field the scenario layer consumes. Point is clipped into its interval, and the count is surfaced as `IntervalForecast.n_point_clipped` so the fix cannot silently mask a bad median model. **Normalization eliminates the crossing entirely (174 to 0)**, an argument for it independent of accuracy.
- **Out-of-season: helps, not enough, pin stands.** Stale Feb-Jun fit into Nov-Dec: **cqr 0.788 to 0.820**, **split 0.817 to 0.831**, at ~38% more width; both still under the 0.85 floor. Open question 4 resolves to leaving `test_coverage_gate_does_not_transfer_out_of_season` untouched, with a companion test recording the partial recovery so the limitation is neither overstated as untouched nor the fix overstated as complete. Rolling recalibration (R2.1d) remains the effective response.
- **Defaults stay opt-in on evidence, not caution:** `normalize_target=False`, because normalization is *not* better everywhere (it hurts at 90 and 180 days), which is the condition the spec set for flipping a default. The **recommended pairing is `normalize_target=True` with a 730-day window**, documented rather than defaulted.
- **New in `bess.forecaster.features`:** `rolling_baseline`, `invert_standardized`; `make_features` gains `normalize` / `season_encoding` / `rolling_stats` (all opt-in, byte-identical when off). **In `evaluate`:** `coverage_by_hour` plus `by_hour` / `max_hour_deviation` on `CoverageResult`. **In `forecast`:** `PriceForecaster(normalize_target=...)` and `n_point_clipped`.
- **Not done (deliberately):** committing; a `references.md` entry for R2.1e (Lei, G'Sell, Rinaldo, Tibshirani & Wasserman, JASA 2018, named from memory as the locally-weighted-conformal candidate and **not verified**; per CLAUDE.md §1 it stays uncited until edition and section are checked, and the affine-inversion argument depends on nothing from it). **Next:** the R2.5/R2.5b/R2.6 value studies still rest on one four-month window of 2024, the same single-window criticism R2.1d fixed for the forecaster, one level up.

## R2.1d IMPLEMENTED (gate green): the walk-forward harness rebuilt, and the R2.1 coverage claim survives it (2026-07-28)

Came out of an audit of every R2.1 choice, from the NL data window to the fold count. The audit found four defects in *how R2.1 was measured*, none in the forecaster itself, so this phase changes only the instrument. **Spec: [`docs/specs/R2.1d-evaluation-honesty.md`](specs/R2.1d-evaluation-honesty.md) (Implemented, every build task and acceptance box ticked). Full suite 335 passed / 27 skipped; live forecaster 8 passed in 67s; fundamentals 1 passed. ruff/format/mypy(43)/lint-imports(4 KEPT)/docs-lint(56) clean. No optimizer change.**

**The four defects.** (1) **"3 folds" was one contiguous fortnight**: the harness took the last `n_folds*test_days` days, so on Feb-to-Jun 2024 every block sat in 2024-05-18..06-01, making every R2.1/R2.1c number a mid-May statement. (2) **The `0.9 ± 0.05` band cited the wrong quantity** (resolved-decision 5 said "calibration-set size"; coverage noise is driven by the **test** set, whose indicators cluster within a day, so at 15 effective days SE ≈ 0.077 exceeded the band). Same category error as the R2.5 VSS gate, fixed the same way: decide with a **day-block bootstrap interval**, failing only when the whole interval falls outside the band. (3) **Sharpness was ungated** (`assert width > 0.0` only), so nothing separated a calibrated forecaster from a wide one, though `walk_forward_pinball_skill` already existed. (4) **The gated model was not the shipped model** (60 / 120 / 200 trees); now unified at the shipped default.
**New in `bess.forecaster.evaluate`:** `Fold`, `rolling_origin_folds` (even spacing across the span, fixed-length rolling training window; refuses rather than overlapping folds or silently shortening a window), `coverage_ci` (day-block bootstrap), `CoverageResult`. Both walk-forward functions share one fold list, so coverage and sharpness use identical blocks. **The R2.1 defaults are preserved exactly** and pinned by a property test, so the historical number stays reproducible.

**The result: the coverage claim survives and is now actually justified.**
- NL **2021-01-01 to 2025-09-30**, 260 evaluated days, shipped capacity: **cqr 0.900** (CI [0.883, 0.916], width 138.3), **split 0.892** (CI [0.874, 0.909], width 160.3). **BE 0.903** (CI [0.886, 0.920]). It now rests on 4.7 years, two zones and a gas crisis. That it did not move is expected for a stateable reason: conformal marginal coverage is self-calibrating, and a rolling window keeps each fold's calibration split adjacent to its test block.
- **The real weakness is conditional, not marginal: per-fold coverage runs 0.617 to 1.000** (cqr). Pooled coverage is essentially exact while individual folds are badly miscalibrated both ways. §R2.1 already listed conditional coverage as out of scope; this measures the cost instead of asserting it, and is the strongest argument for R2.1e.
- **Sharpness, measured for the first time:** conformal pinball **4.999 / 4.541** vs seasonal-naive **22.845 / 16.219**, skill **0.219 / 0.280**. Roughly 4x sharper than baseline, so coverage is not bought with width.
- **R2.1c's fundamentals gain shrinks but holds: −16.6% → −13.4%** pinball (4.770 → 4.132), coverage 0.894 (CI [0.876, 0.912]). The original came from 15 days in one fortnight at 120 trees. R2.1c spec corrected in place, original number kept visible.
- **Rolling recalibration now gated, and it works:** cqr **0.757 → 0.870**, split **0.791 → 0.890** over 34 winter days on a 28-day trailing window, paid in width (98.9 → 184.9). The suite previously pinned the failure of a posture nobody would run and never measured the one `drift.py` prescribes. Stale-fit pin unchanged (**0.788 / 0.816**).

**The training-window sweep answered "one year? two years?", and the first answer was wrong.** Run naively it looked like "longer is better" (coverage 0.843 / 0.855 / 0.900 / 0.891, pinball 5.295 / 5.250 / 4.770 / 3.711 at 90 / 180 / 365 / 730 days). **That is confounded by exactly the defect this phase exists to remove:** a fold cannot start before its training window is full, so each row was scored on a *different* test period, and only short-window rows saw the hard 2021-22 crisis. Re-run on **identical** test days (2023-01-01..2025-09-30): pinball **3.574 / 3.674 / 3.532 / 3.711**, coverage **0.887 / 0.896 / 0.916 / 0.891**, width **96.8 / 100.6 / 107.0 / 106.0**. **Accuracy is flat** (~5% spread, 365 best, 730 worst) and width *grows* with window length. **Resolved: `train_days=365`, on the seasonal-representation argument, not a measured accuracy edge** (it holds every season once and is the precondition for R2.1e's cyclical encoding). **Two years is not worth its 1.5x runtime**, which settles for the raw-target model whether gas-crisis history helps as *training* data: it does not. Whether it helps a **de-levelled** target is the R2.1e question and stays open by design.

**Two findings outside the phase, recorded not fixed.** (1) **The R1.4c guard's stuck-feed rule does not survive long windows:** `guarded_fetch` calls the 4.7-year NL span ANOMALY/`stuck_feed` on a 5-hour run of exactly 64.00 EUR/MWh (2021-05-15) plus 4-hour runs at 42.30 / 140.66 / 95.60, against `DEFAULT_MAX_FLAT_HOURS = 4.0`. Those are ordinary merit-order flats. The nonfocal rule is a **fixed run length applied regardless of window length**, so its false-positive rate grows with the span; it survived only because nothing fetched a window containing such a run (2024 has none, which is why the year-long guard test passes). Same class of defect that forced the *focal* threshold from 8 to 24 hours. R2.1d works around it by fetching the span unguarded (documented in `_span_prices`), since raising a guard threshold to pass a test is the suppression the contract forbids; the fix belongs to R1.4c with its own spec/ADR. (2) **A transient ENTSO-E 503** on a one-year load-forecast request succeeded identically on retry, so it is service load, not a size limit. No loader change.
**Not done (deliberately):** committing; R2.1e; re-running the R2.5/R2.5b/R2.6 value studies on a second window, the same single-window criticism one level up (named in the spec's out-of-scope list). **Unverified reference on record:** Lago, Marcjasz, De Schutter & Weron (Applied Energy, 2021), named from memory as the governing candidate; per CLAUDE.md §1 it stays uncited and `references.md` unwritten for R2.1d until edition and section are checked. The phase relies on nothing from it. **Next:** R2.1e, ordered by this phase's own result: conditional coverage (0.617 to 1.000 per fold) is where the forecaster is actually weak, and de-levelling the target is the change with a mechanism against it.

## R2.1 coverage is an in-season claim: out-of-season fold added, `recalibrate` fixed, cyclical encoding reverted (2026-07-26)

Came out of asking whether 121 days is enough training data. The row count (1915 rows x 8 features reach LightGBM, after the 168 h lag warm-up and the 70/30 conformal split) is not the interesting number: day-ahead is one auction per day clearing 24 hours jointly, so the **effective sample is the day count, 107-117 per fold, about 16 weeks**. Seasonal coverage is the binding limit, not rows.

- **The R2.1 coverage gate never left its own season.** `test_coverage_gate_on_real_prices` trains and tests inside one Feb-Jun window, so its 0.9 ± 0.05 result is a claim about near-term intervals, and nothing in the suite said so. New `test_coverage_gate_does_not_transfer_out_of_season` fits on Feb-Jun 2024 and predicts NL **Nov-Dec 2024**: coverage **0.776 (cqr) / 0.828 (split)** against nominal 0.9, both under the gate band's 0.85 floor. Pinned as a finding (assert `< 0.85`), in the spirit of the R2.4 degenerate-optimum golden; the failure message says that if coverage ever rises into the band the limitation was *fixed* and the claim should be updated, not the bound relaxed.
- **Two causes, and the regime shift is the larger one.** Train mean/std **62.87/35.69** EUR/MWh vs Nov-Dec **110.59/68.25**: roughly double the level and double the spread, so intervals calibrated on the calmer season are simply too narrow. Separately, `month` is a plain numeric feature, so a tree trained on months 2-6 can only split inside that range and resolves `month=12` exactly as it would June. A cyclical day-of-year encoding would at least keep the feature in-range; `hour` and `dayofweek` are fine because training covers their full domain.
- **More history is not obviously the fix**, which is why this is pinned rather than "solved" by widening the window. NL/BE prices are non-stationary (the 2022 gas crisis ran them roughly 10x; continuing solar buildout keeps deepening negative midday prices), so a multi-year window imports dead regimes. That tradeoff is what `forecaster/drift.py` exists to manage.
- **`PriceForecaster.recalibrate` had never worked, and had no test. Now fixed.** Probing whether recalibration recovers the coverage, it raised for **both** methods: `ValueError: conformalize method already called. MAPIE does not currently support calling conformalize several times`, because `fit` had already conformalized that estimator. This is the documented response to exactly this failure (`drift.py:43`: "intervals under-cover: recalibrate, don't retrain"), so that response was unreachable, and no test anywhere referenced the method, which is why it went unnoticed while R2.1's build task ticked it as implemented. **Fix:** `fit` now keeps the fitted base learner(s) and `recalibrate` rewraps them in a *fresh* conformal estimator (`_conformalizer`, both wrappers already `prefit=True`, so no retraining happens) instead of re-`conformalize`-ing the used one. Five new unit tests, **confirmed red first**: it runs and predicts for both methods, the point path is bit-identical afterwards (recalibrating is not retraining), and a deliberately more volatile window really does widen the band (non-vacuity, since "point path unchanged" alone cannot tell a working refresh from a no-op).
- **And the recalibration is worth having: it recovers most of the seasonal gap.** Rolling `recalibrate` on a trailing winter window, fit still frozen at June: **cqr 0.776 → 0.834** (trailing 7 days) **/ 0.883** (28 days); **split 0.828 → 0.888 / 0.885**, against nominal 0.9. Paid for in width, roughly 100 → 160-175 EUR/MWh. A first probe that recalibrated *once* on a 21-day window made coverage **worse** (0.776 → 0.683), which was a strawman rather than a result: one stale conformal quantile fitted on November is not the documented rolling scheme, and November is calmer than December. The out-of-season gate deliberately keeps measuring the un-recalibrated case.
- **Gate status: full suite 323 passed / 23 skipped**; live integration 4 passed for the forecaster file (both gates x both methods) against the warm cache. ruff/format/mypy(43)/lint-imports(4 KEPT)/docs-lint clean. The conformal guarantee also assumes exchangeability, which a price series violates, so the in-season coverage figure is heuristic rather than the finite-sample guarantee the theory advertises.

- **The cyclical day-of-year encoding was tried and reverted, and the reasoning that motivated it was wrong.** Replacing `month` with `doy_sin`/`doy_cos` was supposed to fix the extrapolation above. It does not, for two measured reasons. (1) **The range argument does not survive a partial-year window.** The pair is bounded in [-1, 1] over the whole year, but a Feb-Jun fit only ever sees `doy_sin` [0.503, 1.000] and `doy_cos` [-0.864, 0.861]; mid-December is (-0.276, +0.961), outside **both**. Trees extrapolate exactly as they did with `month`, so the encoding buys nothing the training window does not already deny. (2) **Inside such a window it is the weaker signal:** `doy_sin` turns over at the solstice (doy 92), so one value maps to two dates, while `month` stays monotone and unambiguous there. Empirically out-of-season coverage did not improve (cqr 0.776 → 0.766; split 0.828 → 0.851, inside one standard error of ~0.010) and the **in-season split gate broke**, over-covering at **0.961** against the [0.85, 0.95] band. Reverted rather than rebanded: per the operating contract a red gate means the change is wrong, not the tolerance. The rationale is recorded inline in `features.py` so this is not re-attempted blind.
- **The real fix is the training window, not the encoding.** Cyclical encoding becomes correct once training spans a full year, at which point it removes the artificial December-to-January discontinuity that `month` does have. On a 4-month window no encoding can teach the model a season it never saw. So the two are ordered: widen the window first, then re-apply the encoding and re-measure against the out-of-season gate.

**Next:** widen the forecaster's training window past a full year and re-run the out-of-season gate, which is the only change with a mechanism behind it; re-apply the cyclical encoding *after* that, not before. Weigh it against the non-stationarity above (a multi-year window imports the 2022 gas-crisis regime), so "one recent year" is the shape to test first.

## Price cache switched on: it was built in R1.4b and nothing ever used it (2026-07-26)

Not a phase; a defect found by asking why live runs always re-fetch. **The R1.4b parquet cache worked and was fully tested, but `cache_dir` was opt-in and no production caller opted in**. Every integration test and example called `fetch_day_ahead` bare, so `data/cache/` never even existed on disk and each run re-pulled the same frozen history. The README's claim that the loader "caches to `data/cache/`" was aspirational.

- **The loaders now fall back to `$BESS_CACHE_DIR`** when no `cache_dir=` is passed (`_resolve_cache_dir`, all three of prices / load forecast / wind-solar forecast). Explicit argument still wins, so a test's `tmp_path` is never redirected; unset **or blank** means no cache, so CI is byte-for-byte unchanged. Blank counts as unset because `export BESS_CACHE_DIR=` is how a shell clears one, and `Path("")` would scatter parquet across the working directory.
- **No expiry, deliberately.** A published day-ahead price is a settled auction result and is never revised, and each file is keyed on `(kind, zone, start, end)`, so a hit is correct by construction. The one moving window in the suite (the guard's "last complete month") gets a new key when it moves and re-fetches on its own.
- **Two conftests, opposite jobs.** `tests/integration/conftest.py` points the live tests at `data/cache/`; `tests/conftest.py` clears the variable for every other test, so the suite is hermetic against a developer's exported cache. Without that second one, the unit and property tests that assert an endpoint was called exactly once would silently start reading real parquet and pass for the wrong reason.
- **`test_entsoe_live.py` opts out** (`uses_live_api = True`). It is the watchdog on the live API and its schema; served from parquet it would re-assert that against a file this repo wrote earlier and keep passing indefinitely after ENTSO-E broke. The other live tests cache freely: they consume real prices as *input* rather than testing the transport.
- **Measured, and the headline number is the small one.** Live suite (21 integration tests, token set): **805.66s cold → 769.75s warm**, so roughly 36s of 13.5 minutes, about 4%. That is the honest whole-suite figure and it is *not* impressive, because fetching was never the bottleneck. Per fetch the ratio is enormous (cached parquet read vs live call: **0.077s vs 42.08s** on a full NL year, 0.006s vs 7.72s on 4 months, 0.005s vs 2.74s on 2 weeks, so 500-1200x), but the absolute seconds saved are small next to everything else. Whole cache after a full live run: 14 files, 668 KB.
- **Where the time actually goes, profiled rather than assumed** (`cProfile` over the live suite; profiling inflates it to 1017s). **Pyomo/HiGHS 34.4%** of self time, **LightGBM 0.5%** (4.9s), pandas/numpy 0.1%, and 55.7% in `select.select` I/O wait that cProfile could not attribute (its caller list is polluted by the recursive Pyomo expression walkers, so it is left unclaimed here). An earlier draft of this entry said the suite was "dominated by fitting LightGBM models"; that was asserted without measuring and is **wrong by roughly two orders of magnitude**. The named Pyomo hot spots are model *construction*, not solving: `_add_variables` 71.5s, `dfs_postorder_stack` 37.2s, then `_collect_linear` / `generate_standard_repn`. The suite rebuilds the MILP expression tree from scratch per solve, which is the real target if this ever needs to be faster.
- **The variance is the better argument than the mean.** The same full-year window measured **3.02s in one run and 42.08s in the next**, and during this session one live fetch stalled for **36 minutes** on a hung connection while burning 1s of CPU. So the case for the cache is not the 4%: it is removing a slow, rate-limited, occasionally-hanging network dependency from a suite that is otherwise pure local computation, and making a re-run reproducible against identical bytes. If the suite ever gets faster to solve, the fetch share grows.
- **Gate status: full suite 317 passed / 21 skipped** (311 + 6 new: env-var default, explicit-argument precedence, three blank/unset cases, and the fundamentals pair). The two positive tests were **confirmed red** against the unpatched loader. **Live integration: 21 passed** on both the cold and the warm run. ruff/format/mypy(43)/lint-imports(4 KEPT)/docs-lint clean.

## R2.4 water-value gate FIXED: the invariant was wrong, not the code (2026-07-26)

Surfaced by a newly drawn Hypothesis example at the end of the R2.6 session, then diagnosed and fixed. **The defect was in the property's premise, and neither of the two resolutions first proposed was right.**

- **The failure:** `test_scale_invariance_of_water_value` on `prices=[0,-1,-1,0,0,0]`, `capacity=0.75`, `p_max=2.0`, `η=1`, `soc 0.5/0.5`, `dt=0.5`, `k=2`. Water value at period 1 came back **−1.0 at base scale and 0.0 at 2x**. Not an R2.6 regression: `bess.explain` imports only `assets` and `optimizer`, and this phase touched neither.
- **The actual cause, found by instrumenting rather than reasoning.** The **primal optima differ**. The battery empties at t0 and is then paid to absorb one full charge, which it can take at **t1 or t2** (both clear at −1) for the identical objective. Base charges at t1 and idles at t2; 2x idles at t1 and charges at t2. Objectives scale exactly (0.75 → 1.5), so both plans are optimal. The water value is a property of the **chosen optimum**, so the two plans legitimately report different marginal values at t1: `V*` has a kink there, its subdifferential is the interval [−1, 0], and each plan reports one endpoint. Both answers are correct.
- **So the earlier hypotheses were both wrong.** It is not a false positive in ADR-0023 pinnedness (the tie-break genuinely does not matter at base's t1, where the battery charges), and it is not a wrong dual from HiGHS. Pinnedness detection only sees the ambiguity when it surfaces as a *negative-priced idle* period, which it does at 2x's t1 (correctly reported unpinned, band withheld) and does not at base's t1.
- **The fix, and it strengthens the gate rather than narrowing it.** The property now asserts **unconditionally** that the objective scales by `k` (extensive, always true whatever the solver picks), then compares water values only where the two scales returned the same dispatch. The same kink guard already existed on `test_marginal_value_matches_finite_difference` in the same file; scale invariance had simply never been given it. **No tolerance was widened:** the two values differ by 1.0, not by noise.
- **The discovery is pinned, not excluded.** New golden `test_a_degenerate_optimum_prices_two_plans_differently` locks the instance: objective exactly 0.75 and 1.5, the two scales charging in *different* hours, every reported μ inside the true subdifferential [−1, 0], the μ gap exactly 1.0, and the idling scale disclosing the ambiguity as unpinned with no band. Same spirit as the three-idle-rules oracle: a measured fact the code must not silently change its mind about.
- **`formulation-r2.md` §R2.4 gained the precision** (μ belongs to the chosen optimum; invariance claims about μ hold only where the dispatch is also invariant), with the worked instance and a changelog entry. R2.4 spec's property list amended in place.
- **Gate status: full suite 311 passed / 21 skipped** (310 + the new golden), verified with the Hypothesis example database cleared so the fix is not an artifact of a cached example.

## R2.6 IMPLEMENTED (gate green): price-contingent bid curves, and the value is a null; formulation.md split by release (2026-07-26)

Started from a question about whether the 2 MWh / 1 MW study asset is too small. It is not, and the answer produced a different phase instead. **Spec: [`docs/specs/R2.6-bid-curves.md`](specs/R2.6-bid-curves.md) (Implemented, every build task and acceptance box ticked). Full suite 311 passed / 21 skipped; ruff/format/mypy(43)/lint-imports(4 KEPT)/docs-lint(55) clean. R1, R2.2, R2.3, R2.5 gates untouched.**

**The result, first.**

- **A null, the project's third.** Real NL 2024 (Mar-May, 33 windows, S=10): **BCV median −0.00 EUR at ρ=0.25** (mean −0.40, 30% positive) and **+0.00 at ρ=1.0** (mean +0.42, 12% positive). A price-contingent commitment does not convert into realized euros on this asset. The designed instance proves the mechanism is real (curve value **+28.79 at ρ=0 → +28.54 → +24.69 → +11.80 → +0.00 at ρ=1**, 8 scenarios, T=24), so this is a statement about the market and the scenario set, not a broken implementation. Same mechanism as the R2.5 FV null and the R2.5b tail null: recourse adjusts after the price is known, so anticipating it better buys little. The ρ=0.25 mean is mildly negative, the honest out-of-sample cost of fitting the commitment more tightly to training scenarios. **No figure** (a null needs no chart, the R2.5b call).
- **The delivery gap is the number that is not null, and it is new.** Median **4.00 MWh at ρ=0.25 and 7.91 MWh at ρ=1.0 on a 2 MWh asset**: the commitment promises several times the battery's capacity over a day and does not deliver it. This study does not price that; imbalance settlement would (R3.1). The curve's gap is **smaller** than the scalar's at both budgets (4.00 vs 4.21; 7.91 vs 9.91), so the contingency buys something real, just not euros under this settlement. The most direct argument yet for R3.1 being the right next phase.

**Why not just scale the battery.**

- **Scaling proves nothing, measurably.** `tests/property/test_degradation.py` already pins it: scale `capacity` / `p_charge_max` / `p_discharge_max` by `k` and the objective scales by exactly `k`. Capacity enters `Battery.register` only as a bound on `soc` and a coefficient in the power limits, every variable is indexed over `T` alone, so a 200 MWh model has identical variable and constraint counts, identical solve time, and every euro figure multiplied by `k` (per-unit SoC config, ADR-0009). A bigger nameplate carries zero new information and is actively worse: at tens of MW the price-taker assumption stops being a scoping choice and becomes a modeling error.

**Two conflations found and corrected, one in a canonical doc and one in the proposal itself.**

- **`formulation.md` §R1.1** pointed reflexivity at "the R3 marginal-value bid-curve work", bundling two separable things. **Price impact** (your bid moves the clearing price, endogenous price, residual supply curve) is legitimately R3. A **bid curve** is not a market-power object at all: the clearing price is unknown at gate closure, so what you submit per hour is a monotone set of (price, quantity) pairs and the auction resolves the dispatch. Even a pure price-taker submits a curve, not a schedule, which is uncertainty handling over the R2.2 scenario set and therefore R2. The line now separates them.
- **The proposal's own conflation, caught by reading the code.** R2.3's story treats `π^(s)` as the *realized intraday* price against a separate known `π^DA = π̄`, so conditioning the commitment on `π^(s)` would be anticipativity, not contingency. Resolved by adopting the **auction reading** (`π^(s)` is the uncertain day-ahead clearing price, both legs settle there), the same correction R2.5b already made for value measurement. R2.3 keeps its own semantics.

**The design, and the two gates that are theorems.**

- First stage goes from one shared schedule to one branch per scenario, with two per-hour families: monotone in that hour's price (also an EPEX rule) and equal quantities at equal prices. Together they are exactly the **measurability condition** (they force `g^DA,(s)_t = q_t(π^(s)_t)` for a nondecreasing `q_t`), which is what makes it a bid curve rather than a relabeled clairvoyant program.
- §R2.3 already shows `g^DA` cancels from the risk-neutral expectation, leaving `E[Σ Δt π^(s) g^(s)]`; under the auction reading R2.6 maximizes that *same* expression over a feasible set that nests R2.3's. So collapsed branches reproduce R2.3's objective **exactly** at `λ=0`, and the curve's objective is **provably ≥** R2.3's. At `λ>0` they differ by design (R2.3's fixed-price leg is a forward hedge; an auction leg is not), so the identity is gated at `λ=0` and the difference documented.

**Three defects found by building rather than by review.**

- **A wrong worked example in §R2.6, caught while deriving oracle 3.** The example first written (`π⁽¹⁾=[100,100]` vs `π⁽²⁾=[120,500]`) does **not** bind: branch 1 is indifferent between charging and idling, so it accommodates branch 2 and monotonicity never bites. Replaced with `[60,10]` vs `[80,500]`, where branch 1 strictly wants to sell at hour 1 and branch 2 strictly wants to buy, carrying the exact 185-against-235 arithmetic. The wrong example would have read convincingly in review and produced a golden that passes with monotonicity switched off.
- **A real bug in `_extract_curve`, caught by a property test.** Each step was reported at its *tick-rounded* price, and `np.rint` rounds to nearest, so a step's price could sit **above** a member scenario's own price; reading the curve back (`p ≤ π`) then fell through to the step below and returned a quantity the program never committed. The round-trip property (`curve_response(curve, path_s) == branch_s`) failed on all four seeds and pinned it exactly. Fixed by reporting each step at the **lowest raw price** in its tie group (groups are at least a tick apart, so they stay strictly ordered). Without that property every window in the study would have been silently mis-scored.
- **The off-support caveat is the normal case, not a corner.** A curve's realized commitment is assembled across branches hour by hour, so it is not an R1.1 schedule: over 30 trials (S=10, T=24, held-out paths) it was deliverable **0 out of 30 times**, median terminal-SoC miss **1.38 MWh on a 2 MWh asset** (max 3.43). The binding obstruction is the terminal-SoC **equality**. That made R2.5b's `fix_da` scoring path unavailable and blocked the study.

**Scoring semantics (an evaluation-semantics delta, written into §R2.6 before any code).**

- The commitment is scored as a **cash-flow obligation, not a schedule**: a numeric vector entering only the recourse budget `|g − g^DA| ≤ ρP̄`, with no day-ahead physics block, and the recourse dispatch carrying R1.1. Faithful to what the auction obliges, and it is the §R2.6 settlement under which the commitment only ever acts through the budget. It **agrees exactly** with the §R2.5 path wherever that one applies (an R1.1-feasible commitment), which is what keeps the curve-vs-scalar comparison fair, and its unpriced residual is the delivery gap.

**Solve cost is the phase's limitation, and the obvious fix was measured and rejected.**

- **Measured ladder** at T=24, ρ=0.5: **scalar 0.08 / 0.13 / 0.37 / 0.93 s at S = 5, 10, 20, 30; curve 0.08 / 0.50 / 23.3 / 326 s**, and S=50 never finished. The spec's estimate counted binaries (about 1224 → 2400) and missed the structural cause: **the monotonicity chain couples all S commitment branches within every hour**, so the program loses the block-separability R2.3 has. Growth is steep in S, not the ~2x the binary count suggested.
- **Both levers failed, and saying so is the finding.** Capping the curve at K steps (bucket each hour's prices into K rank-based groups, tie within a group) made it **worse**: S=20, K=5 took **122 s** against 23.3 s un-capped, because tying branches adds equality constraints without removing variables and degrades the relaxation. Relaxing the MIP gap helps but nowhere near enough (S=20 → 12.2 s at `1e-4`; S=30 → 73.5 s at `1e-3`) and would trade away the exactness the oracles rest on. Both probes were scratchpad-only, nothing committed.
- **Resolved: keep one step per scenario, run the study at S=10** (0.50 s per solve). A **stated** approximation, not a hidden one: the R2.5/R2.5b studies default to `n_scenarios=30`, so this study runs at a third of that and reports the S it ran at, costing scenario-set fidelity. **Phase limitation on record: R2.6 does not scale to production scenario counts,** and fixing that is a decomposition problem, not a tuning problem.

**`formulation.md` split by release (decision 5).**

- R2.6 changes the optimizer, so unlike R2.2b/R2.2c it needs a canonical section, and the file sat at 599 of its 600-line cap. `formulation.md` now keeps the preamble, conventions, model at a glance, R1.x, an **index of the R2 sections**, and the changelog for both files; new **`docs/formulation-r2.md`** holds R2.1 to R2.6 and is registered in `lint_docs.py`'s canonical list so the `*Assumes:*` rule covers it. No math moved or changed in the transfer. Every inbound anchor was repointed (glossary, references, README, the R2.1/R2.2/R2.2b/R2.3 specs) plus eleven `src/` docstring pointers; docs-lint's anchor check verifies them mechanically, which is why this was safe in one pass. **Rejected: raising the charter cap**, which would trade a structural fix for a threshold change.

**Code, tests, and decisions.**

- **New in `bess.stochastic`:** `solve_stochastic(bid_curve=...)` (per-scenario `m.da` blocks, `_register_curve_constraints` constraining **adjacent sorted pairs only**, so `S-1` per hour rather than `S²`), `_extract_curve`, `curve_response`, and `solve_stochastic(commitment=...)` for obligation scoring; `bid_curve_value_from_sets` + `bid_curve_value_across_windows` in `study` (token-free, strictly-prior training, infeasible windows skipped not padded). `BidCurveValue`/`WindowBCV`/`curve_response` exported. `fix_da` pins every branch (the collapse); `pi_da` with `bid_curve=True` raises.
- **Interface the tests pin:** `StochasticSchedule` gains `g_da_branches` (S×T) and `curve` (per hour, price-sorted, one step per tied price), both `None` on the default path; under a curve `g_da` carries the probability-weighted branch mean as a **reporting summary only, not a submittable object**. Ties are equality at the 0.01 EUR/MWh tick.
- **Tests, written first and red for exactly one reason.** `tests/golden/test_golden_bid_curve.py` (five oracles), `tests/property/test_bid_curve.py`, `tests/golden/test_golden_bid_curve_value.py`, `tests/property/test_bid_curve_value.py`, token-gated `tests/integration/test_bid_curve_value_live.py`. ## R2.5b IMPLEMENTED (gate green): tail dispatch value measured, a null; R2.2d verdict = do not build (2026-07-24)

Runtime unchanged; mypy clean (Protocol declared read-only via a property so frozen-dataclass forecasts satisfy it).

## R2.2b IMPLEMENTED (gate green): extreme-value (peaks-over-threshold) tail for the scenario bootstrap (2026-07-24)

- **Tests (tests-first):** `tests/golden/test_golden_spike_tail.py` (PWM fit `(ξ,β)=(-1,4)` on `[1,2,3]`; GPD quantile; opt-in + no-exceedance identity; splice-touches-only-exceedances), `tests/property/test_spike_tail.py` (opt-in identity, body-preserved, un-capping, tail-heaviness monotone, valid measure, determinism, reduction-retention, fit-rejects-too-few), token-gated `tests/integration/test_spike_tail_live.py`. Committed figure `docs/figures/example-spike-tail.svg` (`plot_spike_tail` + `examples/spike_tail_demo.py`, synthetic heavy-tail by design; viz + example smoke tests).
